# Decision log

Every modelling choice, why it was made, and its source. This becomes the methodology chapter.
Status: **Fixed** (sourced) · **Assumption** (needs justifying in the thesis) · **Verify** (check before final results) · **Open** (decision pending).
All values live in `config/`; changing one never needs a code change.

| ID | Decision | Why | Source | Status |
|----|----------|-----|--------|--------|
| D-001 | All parameters in `config/default.yaml` + `config/params/`; scenarios override only what differs; each run saves `config_used.yaml` | Reproducibility; every figure traceable to its settings | — | Fixed |
| D-002 | Hourly time step, one calendar year, local time (Asia/Dubai, UTC+4, no DST); timestamps mark the **start** of each hour; sun position taken at hh:30 | Matches tariff and data resolution; avoids half-hour solar offset errors | — | Fixed |
| D-003 | PV: pvlib chain — Hay-Davies transposition, Faiman cell temperature, PVWatts DC + losses + PVWatts inverter. 32 kWp shared array, 25° tilt, south-facing, 5 % soiling | Standard, citable open-source chain. Soiling above pvlib default because of desert dust | pvlib (Holmgren et al., 2018); array size assumed | Assumption (array size, soiling). **Verify:** 2023 check gives pvlib 7.9 % below PVGIS (1,601 vs 1,738 kWh/kWp), low in every month (−3 % Jun to −14 % Oct); target was about ±5 %. Likely irradiance source (NASA POWER vs PVGIS-SARAH3) — compare GHI before tuning losses |
| D-004 | Household demand is synthetic: base + appliance shape + temperature-driven cooling, rescaled to each home's annual kWh and cooling share; four occupancy archetypes; UAE weekend Sat–Sun | No public hourly UAE villa data found yet. Cooling-dominated profile mirrors Dubai reality | Annual kWh (22–46 MWh) and cooling share (65–70 %) assumed | **Assumption — find published UAE villa consumption figures to justify** |
| D-005 | Battery: Powerwall 2 (NMC) default, Powerwall 3 (LFP) scenario; 3 units (40.5 kWh); round-trip efficiency split symmetrically (√RTE each way); both modelled as AC-coupled | Datasheet values; chemistry matches the Schmalstieg (NMC) and Naumann (LFP) ageing papers. PW3 is really DC-coupled to PV — AC-coupled simplification slightly understates PW3 efficiency | Tesla PW2 AC datasheet (AU/NZ); Tesla PW3 datasheet 2024 | Fixed (specs); Assumption (3 units, AC coupling); Verify (PW3 RTE 89 %) |
| D-006 | Tariff: DEWA residential slabs (23/28/32/38 fils per kWh, monthly blocks) + 6 fils fuel surcharge + 5 % VAT, billed **per home** | Real tariff; each villa keeps its own DEWA account | DEWA "Slab Tariff" page | Fixed; Verify fuel surcharge (changes monthly) |
| D-006a | Export treatment is a parameter: `net_metering` (Shams Dubai — exported kWh offset imported kWh at own slab rate, surplus carried forward, no cash) / `no_credit` / `feed_in` | **Key finding:** under monthly kWh net metering the grid acts as a free, lossless battery, so a physical battery *cannot* lower the bill — it only adds round-trip losses. Confirmed on real weather: Brain A costs 204 AED/yr (2023) and 192 AED/yr (2025) more than PV-only under net metering. A shared array serving four separate plots may also not qualify for Shams Dubai, which is plot-based | Shams Dubai net-metering rules | **Open — choose headline case** |
| D-006b | Hypothetical TOU tariff (off-peak 00–07, shoulder, peak 16–22) scaled to be revenue-neutral against DEWA slabs for the default homes | Tests whether the price *shape* makes smart control worthwhile, without changing the price *level* | Own design, clearly labelled hypothetical | Assumption |
| D-007 | Simulation (and forecast test) year **2025**; forecasters trained on **2024**; PV model still checked against PVGIS on 2023 | Realistic day-ahead weather forecasts (Open-Meteo Previous Runs API, value issued 24 h earlier) exist only from 19 Jan 2024. Training on 2024 and testing on all of 2025 gives out-of-sample forecasts with real errors for every simulated hour. PVGIS hourly data ends in 2023, so the PV check stays there | Open-Meteo Previous Runs API; NASA POWER; PVGIS | Fixed (decided 30 Sep 2026). Changes Weeks 1–3 numbers slightly (e.g. Brain A bill 27,152 → 27,250 AED) |
| D-008 | Shared energy split: each hour, community grid import shared in proportion to each home's load; exports credited by ownership share (25 % each) | Simple, transparent default. Week 8 replaces this with the fairness rule | — | Assumption |
| D-009 | Brain A = self-consumption rule: PV → homes → battery → grid; never charges from or discharges to the grid | Standard baseline in literature; matches HOMER's default dispatch closely enough for cross-checking | — | Fixed |
| D-010 | Energy balance checked every hour (AC bus and battery internal); tolerance 1e-6 kWh; run stops on violation | Catches bugs immediately | — | Fixed |
| D-011 | NASA POWER timestamps assumed to mark the hour start; `prepare_data.py` checks this by comparing the daily GHI centre with solar noon | NASA POWER convention not stated clearly in the API response | — | Fixed — 2023 data: GHI centre +0.01 h from solar noon |
| D-012 | Battery temperature: first-order lumped model per unit, C·dT/dt = (T_amb − T)/R + Q_loss, solved exactly each hour; ageing uses the hour-average. Garage ambient = outdoor air lagged 2 h + 2 °C; indoor = fixed 24 °C. C from datasheet mass × 1.0 kJ/(kg·K); R = 10 K/kW per unit | Plan asked for "ambient with a lag plus heat from charging/discharging". Placement changes the temperature the battery sits at for most of its life | Masses: Tesla PW2/PW3 datasheets. Everything else assumed | Assumption (R, heat capacity, garage offset/lag, room temperature). **Open — Powerwalls have active liquid thermal management, which is not modelled; the garage case is therefore pessimistic** |
| D-012a | All battery conversion loss (datasheet AC round-trip, incl. inverter) becomes heat inside the battery | Simple, conservative; inverter heat actually leaves through its own path | — | Assumption |
| D-013 | Ageing = calendar + cycle capacity loss, added. NMC (PW2): Schmalstieg 2014, calendar α(V,T)·t^0.75 (t in days), cycle β(V̄,DOD)·Q^0.5 (Q = Ah per cell), OCV curve of the tested Sanyo UR18650E cell. LFP (PW3): Naumann 2018 calendar k_T(T)·k_SOC(SOC)·t^0.5 (t in seconds), Naumann 2020 cycle k_C(C-rate)·k_DOD(DOD)·FEC^0.5. Changing stress is handled by the "virtual time / throughput" continuation. Resistance growth not modelled | Chemistry matches each product. Parameters copied unchanged from the papers; implementation, units and the 3.149 V floor cross-checked against SimSES source code (TU Munich, BSD-3) | Schmalstieg et al. 2014, J. Power Sources 257; Naumann et al. 2018, J. Energy Storage 17; Naumann et al. 2020, J. Power Sources 451; SimSES v1.3.12 / v2.1.1 | Fixed (models). Verify: compare `scripts/plot_ageing.py` figure with a figure from each paper |
| D-013a | **Cell-to-product transfer.** The models describe the tested lab cells, not Powerwall cells. With Brain A (≈156 cycles/yr, garage, 2025) the NMC model gives 13.0 % loss in year 1 and 80 % health after ≈2.2 years, mostly cycle ageing; the Sanyo cell reaches 80 % after ≈600 full cycles at 80 % depth. LFP gives 4.7 %/yr and ≈18.5 years. Tesla warrants PW2 to 70 % after 10 years | The NMC result is far below real Powerwall life, so absolute NMC lifetimes will be questioned | Tesla PW2 warranty | **Open — choose: (a) report as cell-level results and compare brains relative to each other; (b) scale the NMC cycle term to match the warranty; (c) make LFP (PW3) the headline battery** |
| D-013b | Cycle ageing has no temperature term (both papers cycled at one temperature: Schmalstieg 35 °C, Naumann 25 °C); temperature acts through calendar ageing only | Follows the papers | — | Limitation — state in thesis |
| D-014 | Rainflow counting of the hourly SOC path scores **every** brain after its simulation, identically. Per cycle: depth, mean SOC (→ OCV for NMC), C-rate = depth ÷ hours in which SOC moved. Brain D will optimise a simplified wear cost but is still scored here | Rainflow is not smooth, so it cannot sit inside the optimiser; one scoring method keeps the comparison fair | Plan | Fixed |
| D-015 | Lifetime: `extrapolate` (default) repeats year-1 stress until 80 % health; `multi_year` re-simulates each year with the aged battery (capacity × SOH), looping the same weather year. End of life = 80 % SOH, interpolated within the crossing year; search stops at 25 years | Extrapolation is fast; multi-year shows the effect of a shrinking battery (LFP garage, 2025: 18.5 vs 17.5 years) | — | Assumption (80 % EOL is the usual convention); **Open — which method for the headline tables** |
| D-016 | Open-Meteo radiation is moved 1 h earlier (`radiation_shift_h: -1`); wind converted km/h → m/s; download starts one day early so local midnight (20:00 GMT the day before) is included | Open-Meteo radiation is the mean over the hour *before* its timestamp; this project stamps the hour *start*. Checked: before the shift the GHI centre sits +1.0 h from solar noon, after it 0.0 h; correlation with NASA GHI rises from 0.94 to 0.99 | Open-Meteo API docs; own check | Fixed |
| D-017 | Forecasts are **issued every hour** for the next 24 h (lead 0–23). At issue time t a forecaster may use only: measurements up to hour t−1; the day-ahead weather forecast for each target hour; the calendar. Inputs: forecast weather (GHI, DNI, DHI, temperature and its 6 h mean, wind, cloud, humidity), pvlib run on the forecast weather, clear-sky GHI, hour / day-of-year (sin, cos), weekend flag, same hour yesterday and two days ago, latest measurement and its change vs yesterday, measured 24 h mean temperature. Outputs clipped to physical limits (≥ 0; PV zero at night, ≤ inverter rating) | Brains C/D re-plan hourly over 24 h, so they need a fresh 24 h forecast every hour. Horizon capped at 24 h so "same hour yesterday" is always already measured. A test checks that changing any measurement at or after t leaves the features for t unchanged | Plan; own design | Fixed |
| D-018 | Split by date, never at random. Validation = every 5th ISO week of 2024 (all seasons, ≈20 %), used only for early stopping; training = the other 2024 issue times, dropping any whose 24 h horizon reaches into a validation week; test = every hour of 2025 | Blocked validation keeps whole weeks together (no leakage from neighbouring hours) while covering every season. A tail split (Nov–Dec only) was tried first: winter-only validation stopped XGBoost after 48 trees and gave load RMSE 1.71 kW on the test year, vs 1.38 kW with blocked validation | Bergmeir & Benítez (2012) and Roberts et al. (2017) on blocked validation for time series | Fixed; `validation.method: tail` kept as an option |
| D-019 | Test-year results (2025, RMSE / skill vs persistence): load — persistence 2.33 kW, XGBoost 1.38 kW (41 %), LSTM 1.28 kW (45 %); PV — persistence 1.93 kW, XGBoost 1.33 kW (31 %), LSTM 1.34 kW (31 %). Using the near-perfect "historical" forecasts instead changes these by only ≈2 % (PV LSTM 1.28 kW) | The realistic forecast source costs little accuracy here: most remaining PV error is the gap between weather-model radiation and NASA POWER's satellite values, not forecast lead time | Own runs, `scripts/train_forecasts.py` | Fixed (numbers). **Open — which model's forecasts Brains C/D use** (XGBoost and LSTM are close; LSTM slightly better for load) |
| D-020 | The "actual" load being forecast is the synthetic load of D-004, which is a smooth function of temperature plus random noise. Forecasting it is easier than forecasting real households | Follows from D-004 | — | Limitation — load forecast errors are likely optimistic; state in thesis |
| D-021 | The load model's random noise uses a different seed each year (seed + year) | With one seed, 2024 and 2025 had identical noise patterns, so a forecaster could memorise 2024's noise by day of year and look unrealistically good on 2025 | — | Fixed |
